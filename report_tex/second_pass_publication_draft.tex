\begin{abstract}
This draft reports the second-pass Isaac experiments built on top of the frozen first-pass baseline. The goal of this branch is intentionally narrow: retune and refine the anchored visual--inertial estimator until fused closed-loop performance becomes competitive with visual-only on the clean nominal condition, then test one intermittent-anchor condition and one stronger actuation-stress condition to identify the regime where fusion begins to help. All headline tables and figures in this document are populated from generated artifacts under \texttt{output/isaac\_runs/latest\_second\_pass\_suite/analysis/}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Draft Scope}
The frozen first-pass publication bundle remains unchanged under \texttt{latest\_first\_pass\_suite}. This draft reads only the second-pass suite under \texttt{latest\_second\_pass\_suite}. The scientific question is focused on estimator quality, not on new scenes, new robots, or replay re-solving.

\section{Problem Statement}
This draft studies anchored closed-loop path tracking in a known anchor frame under uncertain servo actuation. The comparison is intentionally narrow: a visual-only estimator and a visual--inertial estimator are evaluated on the same robot, scene, path, and controller, with the second-pass branch devoted to answering when fusion becomes competitive and under which realistic perturbations it starts to help.

\section{Estimator Diagnosis Workflow}
The starting point for this draft is the frozen first-pass publication baseline. That baseline established a complete Isaac workflow, but it also showed that fused uncertainty and residual quality were not yet convincing. The second-pass branch therefore begins with a six-run isolation bundle that separates anchor-only from anchor-plus-auxiliary behavior and keeps the first-pass publication artifacts untouched.

The current branch-level diagnosis is that the main remaining issue is fused mechanization or weighting rather than an auxiliary-tag explosion or a reporting bug. That diagnosis motivates the rest of this draft: tune the fused nominal configuration first, then run a minimal three-condition suite that is still small enough to understand in detail.

\section{Second-Pass Tuning Protocol}
The fused nominal configuration is selected from generated tuning artifacts rather than hand-picked numbers. The tuning sweep uses the same canonical scene, robot preset, duration, and seed throughout, and varies smoother backend and covariance scales only after the estimator-quality diagnostics are in place. This keeps the nominal fused choice scientifically interpretable instead of mixing tuning and benchmark expansion.

The selection rule is conservative. A candidate must complete the trajectory cleanly, stay close to the visual nominal reference on waypoint and position error, and avoid obvious calibration or smoother-feedback pathologies. The resulting fused nominal configuration is then locked and reused unchanged across the draft suite so that condition changes, not parameter drift, explain the results.

\section{Experimental Conditions}
The second-pass draft suite contains three closed-loop conditions. The nominal full-anchor condition establishes the clean baseline. The intermittent-anchor condition uses deterministic anchor-update suppression windows to probe the value of inertial propagation under degraded anchor availability without editing the scene. The servo-stress condition increases actuation corruption while keeping visibility nominal, so the estimator comparison can be read against a harder control regime without changing the environment.

\section{Discussion}
The intended claim of this draft remains deliberately modest. The first-pass suite established a complete Isaac benchmark but left fused residual quality, map quality, and covariance calibration unconvincing. The second-pass branch narrows the problem to estimator quality, tunes the fused nominal configuration against that diagnosis, and then asks whether the improved fused estimator becomes merely competitive on the easy case or actually advantageous once visibility or actuation realism is degraded.

The discussion should be read with that scope in mind. This draft does not claim broad scene generalization, real-phone transfer, replay re-solving, or a final benchmark campaign. It is a focused second-pass closure attempt: one tuned fused nominal configuration, one compact three-condition suite, one artifact-driven paper build, and one local presentation bundle that makes the story inspectable by other people.

\section{Limitations}
Several limitations remain explicit. The suite still uses one scene and one robot preset. Replay remains analysis-only. The intermittent-anchor condition is a deterministic gating schedule rather than a photorealistic occlusion benchmark. The servo-stress condition is intentionally narrow and should be treated as a first draft of the actuation-realism story rather than a comprehensive stress campaign.

Those limits are acceptable for this draft because the goal is to close the minimum publishable second-pass question: whether a tuned anchored visual--inertial estimator becomes competitive with or better than visual-only control once the first-pass estimator-quality bugs are removed, and under which realistic perturbation the benefit becomes visible.
